    02 / BASICS

    Four adjustments worth checking first.

    A — CHAIR

    Support before style.

    Use a stable seat that lets you work without repeatedly reaching or bracing. Adjust height where possible so your working position feels supported.

    B — SCREEN

    Bring the work to you.

    Place the main screen where you can view it without repeatedly craning your neck or leaning forward. Distance and height depend on screen size, vision and task.

    C — INPUT

    Reduce unnecessary reach.

    Keep frequently used keyboard, mouse and tools within an easy working zone. Laptop users may benefit from separating screen position from keyboard position when practical.

    03 / LIGHT
    LIGHT THE TASK,
    NOT THE SCREEN.

    More light is not automatically better. Aim for enough light to see your work comfortably while managing reflections, harsh contrast and direct glare.

    Quick light audit

    1. Look for bright window reflections on the display.
    2. Check whether you squint at either the room or screen.
    3. Move or angle a lamp before buying another one.
    4. Adjust blinds or screen orientation when daylight changes.
    04 / RHYTHM

    Your workspace is also a routine.

    01

    Start clear

    Keep only the tools needed for the current work block in the primary work area.

    02

    Change position

    No single posture needs to be held indefinitely. Build natural opportunities to move and vary tasks.

    03

    Rest your eyes

    Regularly look away from near screen work and let your eyes refocus at a different distance.

    04

    Close the day

    A short shutdown routine—save, note tomorrow's first task, clear the surface—can make the next start easier.

    05 / ORDER

    Cables and small clutter.

    POWER

    Know what is plugged in.

    Avoid damaged cords, overloaded arrangements and cables positioned where they are likely to be pulled or tripped over. Follow electrical product instructions and local safety guidance.

    ROUTING

    Route by movement.

    Keep cables away from chair wheels, feet, doorways and frequently moved equipment. Leave enough slack where devices need to move.

    ACCESS

    Keep useful things reachable.

    Items used every hour belong closer than items used once a week. Storage should reduce repeated awkward reaching rather than simply hide everything.

    06 / REFERENCE

    Workspace language, simplified.

    A small glossary for a very ordinary desk.

    You do not need specialist vocabulary to improve a setup, but these terms help explain common recommendations.

    Neutral postureA comfortable working position that avoids unnecessary extremes of joint position; it is not one rigid pose.
    GlareExcessive brightness or reflection that can make viewing a screen or task less comfortable.
    Reach zoneThe area where frequently used items can be accessed without repeated excessive reaching.
    Task lightingLight directed toward a particular work activity rather than lighting the entire room equally.
    ErgonomicsThe practice of fitting work, tools and environments to people, considering capabilities, limitations and tasks.
    07 / FAQ

    Common desk questions.

    Do I need an expensive ergonomic chair?

    Price alone does not determine whether a chair suits you. Useful factors include stability, appropriate dimensions, support and adjustability for your body and tasks. Try improving fit and setup before assuming the most expensive option is necessary.

    Is standing all day better than sitting?

    DeskWise does not treat one static posture as universally ideal. Alternating tasks and positions can be more practical than trying to remain in one position for an entire workday.

    Where should my monitor be?

    Place it where you can see your main work comfortably without repeatedly craning, twisting or leaning. Exact position depends on the display, your vision, workstation and task.

    What if working at my desk causes persistent pain?

    Persistent, severe or worsening symptoms deserve appropriate professional assessment. This site provides general workspace education and cannot diagnose the cause of pain or other symptoms.
